snapl now watches each device's operational state as well as its configuration (spec 016). Beside the Temporal worker, the worker holds one gNMI subscription per device in intent, every 10 s, for only what intent names: each interface's state and counters, each BGP session end's state and prefixes received, and the device's CPU and memory. It exports these as eight new metric families on its existing endpoint (`snapl_interface_up`, `snapl_interface_octets_total`, `snapl_interface_errors_total`, `snapl_interface_discards_total`, `snapl_bgp_session_established`, `snapl_bgp_prefixes_received`, `snapl_device_cpu_percent` and `snapl_device_memory_percent`), labelled only by device, use case, and the interface or peer intent names. Every 10 s the Observer judges each device's health against intent: `healthy`; `unhealthy`, with one reason per item out of its expected state for more than one interval, such as "BGP 10.10.1.1 active (expected established)"; or `unknown` when no telemetry has arrived for 30 s. A change is recorded as a new audit event, `health_changed`. Health never changes a drift figure or triggers a repair, and CPU and memory never change health. A broken stream is retried with backoff without touching another device, a scan or a deploy, intent is re-read every minute, and `SNAPL_TELEMETRY=off` starts the worker without telemetry. The dashboard shows each device's health beside its drift state: a red "!" or grey "?" on the fabric chip, the fabric's health on the "Devices in sync" card, a red line under each drift lane while a device was unhealthy, and a "Health" section first in the drawer. The drawer also lists the device's interfaces and BGP sessions, flagged when they are not as intent expects, with their traffic, errors, discards and prefixes, and the device's CPU and memory. A folded "The network" metrics group charts traffic by device, errors and discards by interface, sessions established, and CPU and memory, with the note that on the lab these are the host VM's. Any metrics view with more than six series now draws the six highest and says how many it left out. The API adds `GET /api/v1/health` (each device's health and its unhealthy episodes over a range), `GET /api/v1/network` (each device's interfaces and sessions with their latest figures), an optional `health` on each device, six metrics views and the units `bytes_per_second` and `percent`. `snapl status` adds a `fabric health` line that never changes its exit code, and `snapl audit --device` shows health changes. Archives and snapshots carry the health, the network and the six views, and a seven-day snapshot on the fixtures grows from 267 kB to 416 kB. None of this has yet been run against the lab, and until #317 is fixed the lab's eBGP sessions exchange no routes, so prefixes received read 0.
