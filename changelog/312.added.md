The collector can stream a device's telemetry (spec 016). `GnmiCollector.stream_telemetry` holds one sample-mode gNMI subscription per device, every 10 s, for only what intent names: each interface's state and counters (octets, errors, discards), each BGP session's state and prefixes received, and the device's CPU and memory. It yields vendor-neutral samples, and when the stream fails, or stays silent for longer than the dial timeout plus three intervals, it ends by raising `TelemetryStreamError`. The parser is checked by replaying subscriptions captured from leaf-01 and spine-01. Nothing runs it yet: the worker starts the streams in a later PR.
The Observer can judge a device's health from intent and its telemetry: `healthy`, `unhealthy` with one reason per fault ("ethernet-1/49 down (expected up)", "BGP 10.10.1.1 active (expected established)"), or `unknown` when no telemetry has arrived for three intervals. An interface or session must be out of its expected state for longer than one interval before it counts, and an item intent names that the device never reports counts as `absent`. CPU and memory never change health, and health never changes drift.
The worker can export eight new metric families on its existing endpoint: `snapl_interface_up`, `snapl_interface_octets_total`, `snapl_interface_errors_total`, `snapl_interface_discards_total` (each with a `direction` of `in` or `out`), `snapl_bgp_session_established`, `snapl_bgp_prefixes_received`, `snapl_device_cpu_percent` and `snapl_device_memory_percent`. They are labelled by device, use case, and the interface or peer intent names, and nothing else. The device's counters are totals; the worker adds only what each total grew by, starting from 0 and treating a fall as a restarted counter, so a rate never goes negative or spikes. The audit trail has a new event, `health_changed`, which records a device's health before and after, with its reasons.
The worker now streams every device in intent. Beside the Temporal worker it holds one subscription per device, exports each sample's figures, and every 10 s judges each device's health, recording a `health_changed` event in the audit trail when it changes. A stream that breaks is retried after 1 s, doubling to 30 s, and never touches another device's stream, a scan or a deploy. Intent is re-read every minute, so a device added, removed or changed in intent has its stream started, stopped or restarted. When the worker stops cleanly, each device's health is recorded as `unknown`, "telemetry stopped". `SNAPL_TELEMETRY=off` starts the worker without telemetry.
The dashboard and the CLI now say each device's health from the audit trail. `/api/v1/devices` and `/api/v1/overview` carry each device's latest recorded `health` (its state, reasons, since when, and the last sample), or `null` when none was recorded. With Temporal showing no worker polling, a recorded state reads `unknown`, "the worker is not running". The new `GET /api/v1/health` gives each device's health at the end of a range (24 h by default) and its unhealthy episodes over it: an episode opens when a device becomes unhealthy and closes when it becomes anything else, and keeps the reasons it opened with. `snapl status` adds a `fabric health` line, such as `4 healthy, 2 unhealthy (spine-01, spine-02), 0 unknown`, which never changes its exit code, and `--json` adds a `health` key. `snapl audit --device` shows a health change and each of its reasons on its own line. Health events change no drift figure, and every existing route answers as before with them in the trail.
The dashboard has six new metrics views over the worker's telemetry: `interface-traffic` (bytes per second, by device, both directions summed), `interface-errors` and `interface-discards` (per second, by device and interface), `bgp-established` (sessions established per device), and `device-cpu` and `device-memory` (percent). Two units are new, `bytes_per_second` and `percent`. The new `GET /api/v1/network` lists each device's interfaces and BGP sessions as intent names them, with their latest figures from Prometheus: whether each is up or established, traffic, errors and discards each way, prefixes received, and the device's CPU and memory. A figure with no series is `null`, never 0. An interface intent disables is expected `down`, and a session intent disables expects nothing. With Prometheus down the view says why; with the Source of Truth down it lists the devices last seen, without their items. The page puts the six views in a new group, "The network".
The dashboard page shows each device's health beside its drift state, never in place of it. On the fabric panel an unhealthy device's chip gains a red "!" and one whose health is unknown a grey "?", each titled with its reasons, and the legend lists the marks shown; the drift colour and shape are unchanged. The "Devices in sync" card adds the fabric's health to its context ("2 unhealthy.", "All healthy.") without changing its count. The device drawer opens with a "Health" section: the state, since when, and each reason, such as "the worker is not running". Under each device's drift lane a red line marks when it was unhealthy, titled with its reasons, and the lanes' axis starts before the first health change in the range. A snapshot shown at a past time keeps a device's health only from when it last changed.
The device drawer lists the device's interfaces in intent, after what snapl itself found: whether each is up, flagged with a red "!" when intent expects otherwise, its traffic in and out, and its errors and discards under its name when they are not zero. It lists the BGP sessions the same way (established or not, and the prefixes received) and the device's CPU and memory. A missing figure reads "no data", never 0. The page asks `/api/v1/network` once for all devices, every 30 s. With Prometheus down the drawer gives the reason; at a past time it says these show only the present; a snapshot says it does not hold them. In "The network" metrics group, folded by default, traffic reads in bytes per second (`1.2 kB/s`) and CPU and memory in percent (`94%`), with the note that on the lab they are the host VM's. Any metrics view with more than six series now draws the six highest over the range and says how many it left out, where it drew the first six before; the table lists every series, the drawn ones first. Errors or discards that stayed at zero on every interface are said, not drawn.
A history archive now exports `/health` over its seven days, `/network` at its end and the six network views, and opened with `snapl dashboard --archive` it serves them: the network as exported, the health from the archived audit trail. An archive made before this says the network, its views and the health history are not in it. A snapshot bundles the health over its range, the network at its end, and the six views at spec 015's coarse step (one point every 3 h for seven days, said in each view's note), so its drawer and "The network" group work with no server. A seven-day snapshot on the fixtures grows from 267 kB to 416 kB.
